# K4 continuous-time inverse-dynamics torque certification — results (2026-11-18)

Branch `exp/k4-certified-torque-reference-20261117` (research branch; no PR or merge). The frozen K3 qualification campaign, its code, cohorts and `main` were not modified. Hardware: AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (24 threads), RTX 3090 (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1`, used for all timed and generated planner runs), GCC 9.4.0, CPython 3.12 (certification) / 3.11 + torch 2.5.1 (conda `curobo-timeopt-c301cb`, cuRobo pin `c301cb00a87012c2808b1a60649c4065c9b3c95a`).

Evidence classes are kept apart throughout:

- **RIGOROUS (proof-backed):**
  - verified certificates — producer plus independent verifier, outward-rounded interval / Taylor-model arithmetic;
  - verified witnesses — rigorous point enclosures strictly outside a limit.
- **NUMERICAL:** native cuRobo float32 GPU torques compared with the interval model (no FP32 proof).
- **SAMPLED (diagnostic only):** point torques at sample times. Native sampled maxima say nothing about the torque between samples.
- **ANALYTIC:** pendulum and planar-arm closed forms used in P1.

## 1. What is certified

For a TimeOpt output (spline geometry q(s) and timing controls x(s) = ṡ²), the certified torque is

τ(s) = A(q, q′, q″)·x + B(q, q′)·x′ + G(q).

- G = RNEA with gravity.
- B = ½·RNEA(v = 0, a = q′).
- A = RNEA(v = q′, a = q″).

The bound is checked for every s ∈ [0, 1], on the common q/x span partition.

**Certification method.**
- Arithmetic: interval spatial RNEA with outward binary64 rounding, and rigorous sin/cos (Taylor series plus Lagrange remainder).
- Enclosures: first-order Taylor models (tm1) of A, B and G in s, using dual-interval arithmetic. These are combined with Bernstein enclosures of the timing polynomials.
- Refinement: dyadic subdivision.
- Outcomes:
  - **CERTIFIED** — every leaf box lies inside the limits;
  - **VIOLATED_TORQUE** — a rigorous point enclosure lies outside a limit; the witness is replayed by the verifier;
  - **UNKNOWN** — the node or depth budget ran out.

**Contracts** (each reported separately, never pooled):

| contract | dynamics | limits |
|---|---|---|
| MODEL_K3 | (kinematic) frozen K3 compiled producer + verifier | physical velocity, acceleration and jerk |
| MODEL_K4 | frozen cuRobo TimeOpt Panda model, contract v2 `9caa38bf…` | physical FER τ_max 87/12 N·m |
| K3_PLUS_K4 | CERTIFIED iff MODEL_K3 and MODEL_K4 are both verified CERTIFIED on identical bytes | |
| PLANNING_K4 | same model | cuRobo's own 0.95-scaled float32 torque limits |
| IDENTIFIED_K4 | Gaz et al. 2019 identified parameters, contract `4a750169…` (+ hand variant `9fe7f2dc…`) | physical limits; optional friction-bound tightening |
| PAYLOAD_K4 | contract + preregistered HYPOTHETICAL 3 kg / 5 kg payload | physical limits |

A partial kinematic certificate is never promoted to a torque certificate. **No result here is a claim about the real robot's motor torque.** Each result concerns the stated rigid-body model, and the cuRobo model has known physical-validity defects (§3).

## 3. P0 model contract

- **Source.** The contract is `docs/reports/k4/p0/panda_model_contract_v2.json`. It was extracted from cuRobo's `franka.yml` plus the pinned URDF, at the exact decimals the URDF states.
- **Why v2.** cuRobo's float32 rotation matrices are not orthonormal: 3.4e-8 for the hand and 9.5e-7 for the fingers. This made the world-frame and link-frame enclosures disagree at the 3–8e-8 level. Contract v2 rebuilds rotations from the URDF rpy.
- **Physical-validity findings** (reported, not repaired):
  - the link3 inertia tensor is not positive definite;
  - several links share duplicated tensors;
  - several links use 0.01 kg default inertials.
- **Status.** The contract is valid as "the model cuRobo TimeOpt plans with". It is **not** a validated model of the robot.
- **Parity** (`docs/reports/k4/p0/parity.json`, 128 states per set, Pinocchio 4.1.0):
  - Pinocchio, run on its own parameters, lies inside the interval RNEA in 128/128 states for every set. The largest gap was 8.5e-14.
  - cuRobo native GPU torques differ from the interval midpoint by at most 3.7e-6 relative. This is NUMERICAL evidence only.

1. **With a payload, the outputs become torque-limited.** Joint 6 (τ_max 12 N·m) is the binding joint.
   - TimeOpt drives the rigorous point maximum to exactly its own scale: 0.950 / 0.990 of the physical limit.
   - **MODEL_K4 (physical limits, planning model) is CERTIFIED for every eligible payload output (146/146).**
2. **Rigorous between-sample violation of TimeOpt's own planning torque limit** (3 kg, 0.95, `k4-payload-p3-0012-margin_0p950`).
   - PLANNING_K4 is VIOLATED_TORQUE, with a verified witness on joint 6 at s = 189/1102: τ ∈ [11.400565575189976, 11.400565575191717] N·m against the float32 planning limit 11.399999618530273 N·m.
   - TimeOpt's own 512-point enforcement grid stays within the limit (max planning utilisation 0.99995).
   - The dense diagnostic grids do see the excursion: S8193 1.00005, with 12 of 8,193 samples above the limit.
   - The compiled C++ producer independently reproduces the witness at the same point (span 7, u = 15/16, τ ∈ [11.4005655751809, 11.4005655752008] N·m). The violation is 5.7e-4 N·m (5e-5 relative). The output remains CERTIFIED against the physical 12 N·m limit (0.9500 utilisation).
   - This is exactly the failure mode K4 exists to catch: **a limit enforced on samples is not enforced in continuous time.**
3. **UNKNOWN is reported, not hidden** (5 kg, 0.95, `k4-payload-p5-0002-margin_0p950`).
   - PLANNING_K4 is UNKNOWN_DEPTH: depth 12, 1,379 nodes, certified bound 0.99999999.
   - Native float32 samples touch 1.0000002 (S8193). This is not rigorous evidence either way.
4. **Model mismatch.** cuRobo plans with its own model. Under the identified Panda model plus the URDF hand, the same payload trajectories exceed the physical joint-6 limit:
   - 3 kg: 10/40 primary outputs VIOLATED;
   - 5 kg: 16/29 primary outputs VIOLATED;
   - example witness τ₆ ∈ [12.026625452806, 12.026625452808] N·m > 12 N·m.
   This is a statement about the identified model, not a measurement on the robot. **The cuRobo model's torque margin does not transfer to the identified model once the arm is torque-limited.**
5. **No between-sample violations of the physical limit** under the planning model: 0 against the 512-point grid and 0 against all samples.
6. **Duration cost of the payload.** Paired with the no-payload Dev40 output of the same query and arm:
   - 3 kg: median +0.008% (0.95, CI [−0.004, 0.22]), max +7.1%;
   - 5 kg: median +0.44% (0.95, n = 19), with tails up to +100%;
   - 5 kg: 41/70 native failures vs 9/49 without payload.

**Decision.**
- Synthetic payload certification: **GO (exploratory, hypothetical payloads)**.
- 5 kg first-K target: **NOT MET** (29/40). Reported with its true denominator; no extra queries were drawn.
- Payload certification of the real robot: **NO-GO** (no measured payload).

## 8. P5 — compiled pipeline and latency

### 8.1 Pipeline

**Producer** (`k4_certify_path`):
- span extraction from float32 bytes;
- Bernstein enclosures of q, q′, q″, x and x′;
- interval and dual-interval RNEA per box;
- tm1 hull plus remainder;
- dyadic refinement under node and depth budgets;
- rigorous witnesses;
- OpenMP parallelism per span, merged deterministically in span order. The result is bit-identical for 1 and 8 threads (test).

**Verifier** (`k4v_verify`) is independent code. It uses:
- a centred-form enclosure;
- world-frame Newton–Euler;
- its own trig.

It rebuilds the spans from the same bytes, then checks:
- the leaf partition exactly covers [0, 1] per span;
- every recorded box is inside the limits;
- the verifier's own enclosure on each leaf, refined 8 times, is inside the limits;
- the contract hash, the limits being finite, and the input digest.

### 8.2 Tests

`tests/test_k4_compiled_pipeline.py`:
- agreement with Python tm1;
- Python verifier acceptance;
- thread determinism;
- tamper rejection (leaf dropped or duplicated, box zeroed, foreign span, bad path, empty digest, changed timing or limits, wrong status, altered contract);
- witness replay in the Python world-frame verifier;
- budgets and invalid inputs fail closed.

Full suite: 385 OK, 6 skipped (at a913a13).

### 8.3 Independent review (P5b)

An independent reviewer checked the compiled pipeline at a913a13 against a 160-bit Lagrangian oracle evaluated on the exact Python spans. Verdict: **PASS**. Files: `docs/reports/k4_p5b_independent_review/` (`REVIEW_MEMO.md`, `campaign.json`, `run_campaign.py`); tests in `tests/test_independent_k4_p5b.py`.

**Real fixture.** Status agrees with Python tm1 at every limit factor.
- The true joint-4 peak is 29.151767 N·m.
- At a limit of 29.151956 the path is CERTIFIED; at 29.151662 it is VIOLATED_TORQUE.

**Random campaign** (40 float32 paths × 3 limit scales):
- Outcomes: 40 CERTIFIED, 75 VIOLATED_TORQUE, 5 VIOLATED_X.
- Oracle containment at certified leaf points: 1,120/1,120.
- Witnesses confirmed: 80/80.
- Forged certificates accepted: 0/75. The forgeries targeted truly violated paths, with a plausible box and a correct digest.
- Thread mismatches: 0/120.
- Partition and digest bypass attempts were all rejected.

**Findings** (none affects soundness). All were fixed after the review, with regression tests in `tests/test_k4_compiled_pipeline.py::test_p5b_hardening_shapes_physical_gate_digest`:
- **N-P5b-1 (doc):** the REM_SMALL justification comment was wrong (the bound itself was valid); corrected.
- **N-P5b-2:** `CompiledVerifier.verify(expect_physical=True)` now requires exactly the contract effort limits. The latency bench uses it.
- **N-P5b-3:** the producer emits a torque witness only where x ≥ 0 is proven.
- **N-P5b-4:** input shapes are validated (timing length 32, geometry width = dof) in the producer and verifier wrappers, and the shapes are included in the input digest.
- **N-P5b-5 (negligible, not changed):** `bsplit` halving is not outward-rounded for subnormal values. Bernstein coefficients here are far from the subnormal range.

The latency numbers in §8.4 were measured at a913a13, before these hardening changes. The changes touch only input validation, the digest preamble and a witness-emission guard that never fired on these data. After the fixes, the compiled producer and verifier (with `expect_physical=True`) were re-run on all 200 P4 outputs: 200/200 CERTIFIED, 0 verifier rejections. The full suite was also re-run.

## 9. Native FP32 torque (BLOCKED_NATIVE_TORQUE)

- cuRobo's TimeOpt enforces torque on native float32 GPU RNEA samples. There is no FP32 rounding-error proof for those kernels, so native torque values cannot be linked to a certificate.
- Measured agreement (NUMERICAL):
  - ≤ 3.7e-6 relative vs the interval midpoint (P0 parity);
  - native sampled max utilisation minus the rigorous point max ≤ 3.1e-5 over all 600 outputs × 3 grids.
- The payload hook reproduces the payload contract within 3.5e-6 relative.

## 10. P6 — certification-aware retiming

**Program.**
- Fixed geometry, with a sampled TOPP-style program: velocity, acceleration and torque constraints plus linearised jerk.
- Solved by SLP with HiGHS.
- Fallback: λ-backoff toward the native timing.

**Acceptance** requires the full compiled MODEL_K3 proof plus tm1 + verifier MODEL_K4 on the float32 controls. Durations are rigorous time-law brackets, and gains use the conservative ends.

| setting | attempted | accepted (K3+K4 certified) | gain vs native 0.95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| physical limits (1.00) | 40 | 40 | median 3.37% [3.24, 3.62], max 7.04% |
| same 0.95 planning margin as native | 40 | 33 | median −1.1e-5 % (≈0) |

- **The 3.37% gain is consumed margin, not better timing.** At the native's own 0.95 margin, the gain is zero to rounding: TimeOpt is already time-optimal on its own limits.
- Against the native 0.99 arm (a different solve), certified retimed outputs are 0.58% faster (median).
- **7 of 40 were not retimed at 0.95.** The native output itself violates MODEL_K3 at the 0.95-scaled kinematic limits, while K4 is CERTIFIED. The program is infeasible at the native point, and the λ-backoff cannot repair it. These are recorded as no certified retiming.

- **Payload pairing:** an ad-hoc paired-duration computation first matched 0.99 payload outputs against 0.95 no-payload outputs. Corrected to same-arm pairing before reporting, and moved into `k4_analyze_extensions.py`.

## 15. Limitations

- Dev40 is exploratory: n = 40 per arm. No qualification claim is made. With 40/40 certified, the exact one-sided 95% lower bound is 0.928.
- The cuRobo model is physically invalid in places. The identified model has a diagnostic parity gap vs the authors' functions, and friction is handled only as a bound.
- Payloads are hypothetical. Collision geometry of the payload is not modelled.
- Native FP32 torque is not linked to a proof (BLOCKED_NATIVE_TORQUE).
- Without a payload, no Dev40 output is torque-limited. The K4 stress on real outputs therefore comes from the hypothetical payload arms (§7).
