## Contract name and exact statement (scope `T1_AUDITED_FLOAT32_TIMES`)

For ONE frozen native output (NPZ SHA-256), ONE audited executable (SASS SHA-256 recorded by
`scripts/t1_binary_audit.py`) and ONE GPU (name and compute capability recorded):

1. **Reported values (production).** For every float32 requested time t whose bit pattern lies in
   `0x00000000 .. bits(T_native)` (+0, every positive subnormal and normal, endpoint included;
   `T_native = time[intervals]`, the production non-tail domain), the q, v, a, j words written by
   the *production* `sample_with_progress_kernel`, launched through cuRobo's own launcher
   (`trajectory_timeopt.sample`), are all finite. If no v/a/j word exceeds its physical limit
   (`min(|lower|,|upper|)`, compared exactly in float32), then the status is
   `REPORTED_WITHIN_LIMITS_ALL_FLOAT32_TIMES`. Otherwise every exceedance is counted and the
   status is `REPORTED_EXCEEDS_LIMITS`.
2. **Time inversion.** For every t in the same domain, and additionally in `(T_native, T_exact_hi]`
   when the native duration is shorter than the certified exact duration:
   `|s_native(t) − s*(t)| ≤ ε_s`. Here `s*` is the exact inverse of `T(s)=∫_0^s x^{-1/2}`, held at 1
   after the model duration. ε_s is a certified upper bound: the far-end distance
   `max(s−lo, hi−s)` to a rigorous bracket `[lo, hi] ∋ s*(t)`. The window ends at the smallest
   float32 ≥ `T_exact_hi`. Beyond it s* = 1 and production progress saturates at 1. Inside the
   window the production v/a/j are the zero tail, so the `L·ε_s` statement there concerns the
   exact-model values only, not the reported arrays. The q, v, a, j of the exact model
   at `s_native(t)` then lie within `L·ε_s` of their values at `s*(t)` (per-joint rigorous Bernstein
   Lipschitz bounds; jerk adds one knot jump when within ε_s of a geometry knot).
3. **Linkage.** The harness progress used in item 2 is linked to production because all 4·dof
   output words of harness and production are bitwise identical at every enumerated time. This is
   evidence of identical computation, not a proof that the hidden production progress word is
   equal.

Not claimed:
- Behaviour between float32 times. The native API specifies no interpolation or hold.
- That the reported v/a/j arrays are physical time derivatives of the commanded sample stream.
- Robot tracking, dynamics, torque or collision.
- Other GPUs, other binaries, or unseen trajectories.

The audit is offline. Its cost (about 9 to 10 s per output on the RTX 3090) is never part of the
online certification latency.

**Excluded domain (production semantics, from source `timeopt_path_kernel.cuh`):**
- −0.0 is equivalent to +0, which is audited. `requested < 0` is false for −0, so it does not take
  the tail branch, and its outputs are bitwise identical to +0.
- Negative t: tail, so v=a=j=0 and q is taken at the clamped progress.
- t > T_native: tail, so v=a=j=0 exactly.
- NaN/Inf: production writes NaN.

These are documented, not enumerated. Item 2 covers the inversion up to `T_exact_hi`.

## R0 verdict

- **Round 1:** FAIL. The bound was unsound (U1).
- **Round 2:** PASS for using v2 in the formal cohorts
  (`docs/reports/r0_independent_t1_review/round2/REVIEW_MEMO_ROUND2.md`). It has three conditions:
  - Freeze the reviewed hashes.
  - Accept an output only when its status is `AUDITED_CONSISTENT`.
  - Apply wording fixes D1–D3.

D1–D3 are applied:
- **D1:** −0 wording.
- **D2:** separate `REPORTED_NONFINITE` label.
- **D3:** the window stop is decided in exact binary64. The NumPy NEP 50 float32 comparison never
  fired, which was sound but did not match the comment.

D2 and D3 change the driver, so the reviewer confirmed the final diff before the freeze.
